Walder 0.1.2 · new-user handbook

Walder, and what he is telling you Every face, wiggle and speech bubble the desk dog has — what each one means, and the five minutes of setup that has to happen first.

The idea in one line

Walder has exactly one job: tell you how much of your Claude 5-hour window is gone without you having to ask. His face follows that single number. His bark names any window that has just crossed a threshold. Everything else he does is either a reaction to you, a reaction to Claude Code, or him getting out of your way.

Phase A · Before he can tell you anything

Five minutes, once

A1

Know what you are installing

Walder is a small pixel-art dachshund who sits on top of your desktop and watches how much of your Claude and ChatGPT allowances you have left. He is not a window and not an app you switch to.

There is no window, no Dock icon and no settings screen. Two things exist: a bone icon in the menu bar, which is the entire app, and the dog himself, who starts in the bottom-right corner of your main screen.

Note

He talks to claude.ai, api.anthropic.com and chatgpt.com, and nowhere else. No analytics, no account, no server. Logins are read at the moment of a check, held in memory, and never written to disk or into the log.

A2

Get past the security warning (Mac)

Open the .dmg, drag Walder.app into Applications, then double-click it. macOS will refuse to open it — that is expected, and it is not a sign of a broken build.

Go to System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security, scroll to the message about Walder being blocked, and click Open Anyway. Confirm with your password or Touch ID.

Double-check

On macOS 15 and newer, right-click ▸ Open no longer works — it gives you the same refusal as a double-click. Open Anyway in System Settings is the only route that works on every version. Walder is unsigned (no Apple developer certificate), so macOS cannot check who made it. This is a one-time step.

A3

If you are on Windows, expect to be the first

Run the .exe, click More info on the SmartScreen warning, then Run anyway. The installer asks where to put it and offers a desktop shortcut.

Double-check

No Walder developer has ever run the Windows build — not the installer, not the tray icon, not the fullscreen detection. It is expected to work; nobody has watched it. If you are the first, please report what you actually see, including the parts that go fine.

A4

Find him on first run

Nothing appears in the Dock or the taskbar. Look for the bone icon in the menu bar (Mac, top right) or the system tray (Windows, bottom right), and for the dog in the bottom-right of your main screen.

He is drawn on a 72 × 72 grid and shown at Medium by default — 144 screen pixels, which is the size the artwork was made for.

This is him doing nothing at all

Breathing, four frames, half a second a lap. If this is what you see, nothing is wrong and nothing is happening.

replay

always on

idle

4 frames · 125 ms · loops · 0.50 s a lap

The base loop. Frame 3 lifts his head — that small rise is the whole difference between a picture of a dog and a dog.

A5

Log in, or he cannot tell you anything

From the bone icon: Accounts ▸ Claude ▸ Log in…, and then Accounts ▸ ChatGPT ▸ Log in…. A normal login window opens on the real site; log in as you would in a browser. The window closes itself once the login has taken.

Double-check

Until a Claude login exists, Walder shows the confused head-tilt with a ? and nothing else. That face is honest, not broken — it means "I have no number for you". He will never show a cheerful face on a number he does not have.

Note

If you already use Claude Code or the Codex CLI on this machine, Walder can read those logins by itself and may show numbers before you log in to anything.

A6

Optional: let Claude Code poke him

Install Claude Code hooks… in the menu — only if you use Claude Code in a terminal, and only if you want him to react to it. It asks first, names the file, writes three small entries into ~/.claude/settings.json, and tells you what it did. Your original file is copied first, and Remove Claude Code hooks… undoes it.

What you get: ears up and a woof when Claude Code finishes a reply, and a head tilt with a ? when it is waiting for you.

Note

The hooks send a tiny message to a listener that only accepts connections from your own machine, normally on port 47811. If Walder is not running, the entries do nothing and Claude Code carries on exactly as before.

A7

Tick Launch at login

Last item of setup: Launch at login in the menu, so he comes back after a restart. The numbers then refresh by themselves about every three minutes, forever, with no further involvement from you.

Double-check

The first four are the same drawing. In v0.1.2 idle_happy, idle_neutral, idle_worried and idle_exhausted all point at the same four frames, so anything under 95 % looks identical — there is no grin, no ears-down, no sweat bead yet. Only out and confused are visually distinct. So: read the number off the hover card, not off his mood, until the missing poses are drawn. What you can trust with your eyes: he is flat on the floor (spent), or he is cocking his head (no data).

Note

Why: every frame is one of Victor's thirteen strip illustrations, used 1:1 with nothing redrawn — and none of the thirteen contains a worried or panting dog. The code is ready for the poses; the drawings do not exist yet.

B3

When he barks, and what the bark says

A bark is one short animation plus a speech bubble. It fires when a usage window crosses 80, 85, 90, 95 or 100 % — once per threshold per window, so he does not nag. Every window he can see gets its own barks, not only the 5-hour one.

The bark

Four frames, 400 ms. Frame 3 is mouth open with motion lines.

replay

a threshold crossed

bark

4 frames · 100 ms · plays once · 0.40 s

The bubble names the window and the **real** reading, not the threshold that fired: cross 80 % at 87 % and it says 87% used.

The bubble readsMeaning
5-hour: 87% usedYour Claude 5-hour window. This is the one that drives his face.
7-day (all models): 91% usedThe weekly pool, across every model.
7-day Fable: 91% usedThe same weekly pool seen under the Fable name — shown because the dashboard shows it. Marked (shared pool) on the card, and it never barks twice about the same number.
Codex 5-hour: 90% usedA ChatGPT / Codex window. Labels come straight from whatever the service reports, so a new window can appear without a new version of Walder.
Note

A bark stays up for about 12 seconds, then goes by itself — or straight away if you click him. If two windows cross at the same moment, the more important one is shown and the other waits its turn.

B4

The four things a bubble can be

5-hour: 87% used

A bark. A real threshold crossing. 12 seconds, then gone. This is the only kind that carries a number.

woof

A perk. Claude Code just finished a reply. 5 seconds. Only exists if you installed the hooks.

?

Waiting. Claude Code wants something from you — a permission question, or an idle prompt. No time limit: it stays until you type your next message or click him.

…zzz

A sleepy mumble. You petted him while he was curled up asleep. 1.5 seconds, and he stays asleep. It is an acknowledgement, not a message.

Note

A bark outranks a woof and a ?: if one arrives while either is up, the bark takes the screen and the woof is not re-queued afterwards. A woof that has already been seen has done its job; a warning shown five seconds late is a warning shown after the fact.

B5

He gets out of the way for fullscreen video

Curling up, and standing back up

Two animations, and the size of the window changes with them: the curled pose has its own smaller 61 × 58 box, so a sleeping Walder is visibly a smaller dog.

replay

something went fullscreen

sleep

3 frames · 1000 ms · loops · 3.00 s a lap

A slow three-second loop. Frame 3 carries the drawn z z — which is why the app does not also print its own.

replay

fullscreen ended, or he has something to say

wake

4 frames · 125 ms · plays once · 0.50 s

Curled, yawn, stretch, shake. Also plays when a bark needs saying while he is asleep — he wakes up long enough to say it.

It is per screen: a film on your second monitor does not put a dog sitting on the laptop screen to sleep. To switch it off, untick Sleep during fullscreen video — he then stays visible over everything.

Double-check

On Windows this leans on a small PowerShell helper nobody has ever watched run. If he never sleeps there, that is the first thing to put in a report. There is also one accepted false positive on the Mac: Dock hidden plus a maximised window can read as fullscreen, and he will curl up when no video is playing.

B6

The two Claude Code reactions

Only if you installed the hooks

Both of these hold their last frame — the pose has to stay while the bubble that goes with it is still on screen.

replay

claude code finished a reply

perk

3 frames · 100 ms · plays once, then holds · 0.30 s

Resting, lifting, head high with ears flared — then it parks there for the 5 seconds the woof is up.

replay

claude code is waiting for you

tilt

3 frames · 125 ms · plays once, then holds · 0.38 s

Head cocks over, and frame 3 carries the drawn ?. It holds until you type your next message or click him — there is no timer.

Note

The held head-tilt is also exactly what the confused face is: the same last frame, looped on its own. Same picture, two different reasons — a ? next to a dog means "something is waiting on you" if you use Claude Code, and "I have no number" if you have not logged in. The hover card tells the two apart in one glance.

Phase D · Living with him

Four gestures, two settings, one menu

D1

The four things you can do to him

Do thisYou get
HoverAfter a moment, a card appears beside him: every percentage, how long until each one resets, where each number came from, and how old it is. This is the authoritative read — his face is only a summary.
ClickHe squeezes his eyes shut and a heart pops up. Also dismisses whatever bubble is up, and asks for a fresh check (at most once a minute).

D2

Size and coat

Size: Small, Medium or Large — one, two or three screen pixels per drawn pixel, so 72 px, 144 px or 216 px.

Phase E · When he looks wrong

Read the card first, then the menu

E1

Start with the hover card — it names the problem

Almost every confusing state has its explanation written on the card. Hover him before touching anything.

The card saysWhat to do
a login or auth line, e.g. auth-neededAccounts ▸ Log in… for that service. If it was working yesterday, Log out first, then log in again.
endpoint-changedThe service moved something Walder reads. Nothing you can fix — and the other numbers still work.
rate-limitedYou asked too often, or the service is throttling. It backs off and retries by itself.
the age line looks old, or not checked yetRefresh now. Stale numbers are marked rather than hidden — they are still the best information there is.
a Fable row equal to the All-models rowCorrect, not a bug. Fable draws from the shared weekly pool, so it is the same number under the name you recognise. It is marked (shared pool) and never barks twice.
E2

The four everyday complaints

SymptomFix
He is confused and stays confusedNo Claude login. Accounts ▸ Claude ▸ Log in…. He is being honest, not broken.
I cannot find himReset position. He is probably on an unplugged monitor, or behind a window at the Small size.
My clicks near him land in the wrong placeTick Force interactive (debug), do what you needed to do, untick it.

Double-check

He is asleep and there is no fullscreen video. Untick <b>Sleep during fullscreen video</b>, which wakes him at once. Then say so in a report: the likely cause is the known false positive (Dock hidden plus a maximised window), and it is worth knowing how often it bites.

E3

If it is something else, send the log

Tick Developer ▸ Verbose log, reproduce the problem, then send the file. Warnings are always written; the tick adds the detail. It rotates at 1 MB and keeps three files, so it cannot fill a disk.

PlatformLog file
Mac~/Library/Logs/walder/walder.log
Windows%APPDATA%\walder\logs\walder.log — untested; trust the path the menu prints
Note

Logins and tokens are stripped from anything written to that log — anything that looks like one is masked — so the file is safe to send as-is.

Phase F · Appendix: why he did that

The arbitration rules, in one page

F1

Who wins when several things happen at once

Four things compete for one small dog. This is the order they resolve in — it explains almost every odd moment you will see.

1

A usage bark wins outright. It takes the screen from a live woof or head-tilt, and that one is not put back afterwards.

2

A bark wakes him. If a threshold is crossed while he is curled up asleep, he stands up, says it, and curls back down when the bubble clears.

3

Claude Code events queue, and the newest wins. At most one woof and one ? are ever waiting, so a burst of replies cannot back up into a minute of bubbles.

4

Fullscreen only takes effect once the screen is clear. Starting a film while a bubble is up changes nothing until that bubble goes; then he curls up.

5

Petting a sleeping dog does not wake him. He mumbles …zzz and stays curled — the one bubble that does not count as "something to say".

6

His face changes silently. A new percentage swaps the idle loop with no animation and no bubble, and the same face is never re-sent twice in a row.

Note

If you ever want to watch the whole vocabulary at once, a developer can run npm run sprites — the gallery that shows every animation at 4× with its frame count and durations, a coat switcher and a play-once button. It is how the artwork gets approved in the first place.

Quick sanity checklist

  • The bone icon is in the menu bar, and the dog is on screen.
  • Accounts ▸ Claude is logged in — he is not showing the confused head-tilt.
  • Accounts ▸ ChatGPT is logged in, if you use it.
  • Hovering him shows real percentages with reset times, and the age line is fresh.
  • Launch at login is ticked.
  • Hooks installed only if you use Claude Code — and his ears went up on the next reply.
  • You know that under 95 % his face all looks the same, so the hover card is the real number.
